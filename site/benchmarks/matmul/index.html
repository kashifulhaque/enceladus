Benchmarks

Matmul

Matrix multiply throughput for both tl.dot backends and the autotuned kernel, across four shapes and three dtypes, against MLX and PyTorch.

What it measures

bench_matmul multiplies an M × K matrix by a K × N matrix from device memory with the descriptor matmul in examples/04_matmul.py. It runs the following shapes (M × N × K) in float32, float16, and bfloat16:

  • 4096 × 4096 × 4096, a large square matmul.
  • 2000 × 2000 × 2000, whose size isn't a multiple of the tile size.
  • 513 × 513 × 513, a small matmul with a one-element ragged edge.
  • 1024 × 4096 × 1024, a rectangular output.

For each shape and dtype, the script times the following Enceladus variants:

  • simdgroup: a fixed 64 × 64 × 32 tile over 4 SIMD groups, on the simdgroup_matrix backend.
  • mpp: a fixed 64 × 64 tile over 4 SIMD groups, on the Metal 4 matmul2d backend.
  • Tuned: matmul_tuned, which autotunes over matmul_configs on first use. The table names the configuration it picked.
  • Fused bias and GELU: examples/07_matmul_fused.py on both backends, at 4096³ in float32 and float16 only.

The variants run in 3 interleaved rounds of 10 GPU-timed runs, and each cell reports TFLOPS at the minimum and the median time. MLX (a @ b) and PyTorch MPS (a @ b) report the minimum of 10 wall-clock runs after 3 warmup runs.

Reading the results

  • At 4096³, Enceladus is within a few percent of MLX. MLX leads in float32 (5.19 against 5.09 for simdgroup) and float16 (5.77 against 5.70 for mpp). Enceladus leads in bfloat16 (5.73 against 5.59). At this size, the wall-clock overhead in the MLX and PyTorch times is under 1%.
  • float32 mpp is bimodal at 4096³. Its minimum, 5.35, is the fastest float32 result, but its median is 3.36, and the fused mpp variant shows the same pattern at 3.60 (3.22). Earlier sweeps of tile shapes and K steps found nothing faster than simdgroup for this case, and tuning picks a simdgroup configuration.
  • mpp wins on ragged shapes. At 2000³, mpp is 16-23% faster than simdgroup, and it beats MLX by 6% in float16 and 9% in bfloat16, more than the overhead of about 3% at this size. In float32, the tuned kernel and MLX are even, at 4.77 and 4.81.
  • 513³ favors small tiles. The tuned 32 × 32 mpp kernel reaches 3.75-4.32 TFLOPS, more than twice the fixed 64 × 64 simdgroup kernel. A 32 × 32 tile splits the output into 289 programs instead of 81, and wastes less work on the ragged edge. MLX and PyTorch measure 1.29-1.68 TFLOPS here, which is 0.16-0.21 ms per matmul, so the roughly 0.1 ms of wall-clock overhead is up to half of their times. Part of the 2.7-3.1× gap is measurement, not kernel speed.
  • The lead at 1024 × 4096 × 1024 is about the size of the overhead. Enceladus leads MLX by 8-10%, for example 6.09 against 5.53 TFLOPS in float16. That's a difference of about 0.14 ms on a 1.4-1.7 ms matmul, so most of the lead can come from the wall-clock overhead.
  • Fused epilogues cost little on float16. Bias plus GELU on mpp runs at 5.65 TFLOPS against 5.70 for the plain mpp matmul.
  • The autotuner picks a near-best configuration. Each tuning run is short, so the tuned kernel lands within about 6% of the fastest fixed variant, not counting the bimodal float32 mpp. For example, it measures 4.81 against 5.09 for simdgroup at 4096³ in float32. The tuned and fixed simdgroup kernels at 1024 × 4096 × 1024 in float32 share a 4.79 median but differ by 5% in their minimums, which shows the run-to-run noise.

For how to choose a backend and autotune a matmul, see Performance. For the tl.dot arguments, see the language reference.
