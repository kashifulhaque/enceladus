Benchmarks

Matmul

Matrix multiply throughput for both tl.dot backends and the autotuned kernel, across four shapes and three dtypes, against MLX and PyTorch, plus an exact int8 matmul.

What it measures

bench_matmul multiplies an M × K matrix by a K × N matrix from device memory with the descriptor matmul in examples/04_matmul.py. It runs the following shapes (M × N × K) in float32, float16, and bfloat16:

  • 4096 × 4096 × 4096, a large square matmul.
  • 2000 × 2000 × 2000, whose size isn't a multiple of the tile size.
  • 513 × 513 × 513, a small matmul with a one-element ragged edge.
  • 1024 × 4096 × 1024, a rectangular output.

For each shape and dtype, the script times the following Enceladus variants:

  • simdgroup: a fixed 64 × 64 × 32 tile over 4 SIMD groups, on the simdgroup_matrix backend.
  • mpp: a fixed 64 × 64 tile over 4 SIMD groups, on the Metal 4 matmul2d backend.
  • Tuned: matmul_tuned, which autotunes over matmul_configs on first use. The table names the configuration it picked.
  • Fused bias and GELU: examples/07_matmul_fused.py on both backends, at 4096³ in float32 and float16 only.

MLX (mx.eval(a @ b)) and PyTorch MPS (a @ b followed by torch.mps.synchronize()) run in the same 3 interleaved rounds as the Enceladus variants. In each round, every Enceladus variant runs 10 times with GPU timestamps, and then MLX and PyTorch each run 3 warmup calls and 10 wall-clock runs. Every cell reports TFLOPS at the minimum and the median of the 30 samples. Because all of them share the rounds, a change in the GPU clock during the run affects each one alike.

The script also times an exact int8 matmul with int32 accumulation at each shape, with three fixed tiles, and reports TOPS. MLX's matmul takes only floating-point types, and PyTorch MPS int8 matmul returns int8, so neither has a comparable int8 number.

Reading the results

  • At 4096³, Enceladus matches or leads MLX in the same rounds. mpp leads MLX by 3% in float16 (6.04 against 5.88) and in bfloat16 (6.06 against 5.86). In float32, simdgroup and MLX are even, at 5.27 and 5.21, and PyTorch is fastest at 5.42. At this size, the wall-clock overhead in the MLX and PyTorch times is under 1%.
  • float32 mpp varies between rounds. At 4096³, its minimum is 5.40 but its median is 4.69, and the fused mpp variant measures 5.48 (4.78). float32 matmul2d throughput swings by 20% or more from one round to the next, while simdgroup and MLX hold steady in the same rounds. Apple's own matmul2d reference kernel, run without Enceladus, swings the same way. Grouped (swizzled) program orders don't help: the hardware's 2D dispatch order already gives cache reuse, and a plain row-major 1D order drops float32 matmul2d to 2.2 TFLOPS. float16 and bfloat16 mpp are stable, with medians within about 2% of their minimums.
  • mpp wins on ragged shapes. At 2000³, mpp is 14-17% faster than simdgroup, and the best Enceladus kernel beats MLX by 8-9% in every dtype, for example 5.86 against 5.40 in float16. That's more than the wall-clock overhead of about 3% at this size.
  • 513³ favors small tiles. The tuned 32 × 32 mpp kernel reaches 3.74-4.31 TFLOPS, more than twice the fixed 64 × 64 simdgroup kernel. A 32 × 32 tile splits the output into 289 programs instead of 81, and wastes less work on the ragged edge. MLX and PyTorch measure 1.42-1.62 TFLOPS here, which is 0.17-0.19 ms per matmul, so the roughly 0.1 ms of wall-clock overhead is about half of their times. Part of the 2.5-2.8× gap is measurement, not kernel speed.
  • The lead at 1024 × 4096 × 1024 is about the size of the overhead. Enceladus leads MLX by 9-12%, for example 6.05 against 5.47 TFLOPS in float16. That's a difference of about 0.15 ms on a 1.4-1.8 ms matmul, so most of the lead can come from the wall-clock overhead.
  • Fused epilogues cost at most 1% in float16. Bias plus GELU runs at 6.00 TFLOPS on mpp, against 6.04 for the plain mpp matmul, and at 5.73 on simdgroup, the same as the plain simdgroup matmul.
  • The tuned kernel matches the best fixed variant. In this run, the tuned kernel is at least as fast as the fixed simdgroup kernel for every shape and dtype, within noise. At 4096³ in float32, it picked simdgroup 64 × 64, the fixed configuration itself, and in float16 and bfloat16 it picked mpp 64 × 64. In four separate tuning runs with an empty cache on a cool GPU, the tuner picked mpp 64 × 64 at 4096³ for float32, float16, and bfloat16 every time. Those picks beat the fixed simdgroup configuration by 3-6% and matched the fixed mpp configuration within 1%.
  • Exact int8 runs below float16. The fastest int8 tile reaches 4.75 TOPS at 4096³ and 4.83 TOPS at 1024 × 4096 × 1024, against 6.04 and 6.05 TFLOPS for float16 mpp. The 64 × 64 × 64 tile is fastest, or tied for fastest, at every shape except 513³.

For how to choose a backend and autotune a matmul, see Performance. For the tl.dot arguments, see the language reference.
