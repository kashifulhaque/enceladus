Benchmarks

Normalization

LayerNorm and RMSNorm forward bandwidth on a 4096 × 4096 matrix, using the kernels from the examples.

What it measures

bench_norms runs two example kernels on a 4096 × 4096 matrix in float32 and float16, one row per program, with 8 SIMD groups:

  • LayerNorm from examples/03_layernorm.py, with BLOCK=1024 and BLOCK=4096. In the style of the Triton tutorial, the kernel reads each row three times: once for the mean, once for the variance, and once to normalize. It also writes the per-row mean and reciprocal standard deviation.
  • RMSNorm from examples/06_rmsnorm.py, with BLOCK=4096, which holds the whole row in one block.

Times are the minimum of 20 GPU-timed runs. GB/s counts the minimum traffic, one read and one write of the matrix, so the LayerNorm rereads don't count. The script has no MLX or PyTorch comparison.

Reading the results

  • RMSNorm runs at copy bandwidth. At 239.5 GB/s in float32 and 247.4 GB/s in float16, it's close to the row softmax on the same matrix.
  • LayerNorm's rereads mostly hit the cache. With BLOCK=1024, LayerNorm reaches 237.4 and 241.8 GB/s, within 1-3% of RMSNorm, even though it reads each row three times.
  • Block size matters little. In float32, BLOCK=4096 costs about 1% against BLOCK=1024: 234.4 against 237.4 GB/s. In float16, the two block sizes run at the same speed, 241.9 and 241.8 GB/s.

For the kernels themselves, see the LayerNorm and RMSNorm pages in Examples.
