Benchmarks

Softmax and reductions

Row softmax bandwidth on a 4096 × 4096 matrix against MLX and PyTorch, and a row-sum reduction across threadgroup sizes.

What it measures

bench_softmax defines its own two kernels and runs them on a 4096 × 4096 matrix, one row per program with BLOCK=4096:

  • Row softmax in float32 and float16, computed in float32, with 4, 8, and 16 SIMD groups (num_warps). It compares against mx.softmax(x, axis=-1) and torch.softmax(x, dim=-1) on MPS. GB/s counts one read and one write of the matrix.
  • Row sum in float32 with 4, 8, 16, and 32 SIMD groups, that is, 128 to 1,024 threads per threadgroup. GB/s counts one read of the matrix. This checks whether reductions stay fast as the threadgroup grows.

Enceladus times are the minimum of 20 GPU-timed runs. MLX and PyTorch times are the minimum of 20 wall-clock runs after 10 warmup runs, so they include about 0.1 ms of launch and synchronization overhead.

Reading the results

  • Softmax runs at copy bandwidth. Enceladus reaches 238.1-256.3 GB/s, at or above the vector add's bandwidth, so the max, exponent, and sum cost little on top of the memory traffic. The SIMD-group count changes the result by about 3% at most.
  • The lead over MLX is mostly overhead. Enceladus reports 25% more bandwidth than MLX in float32 and 50% more in float16. Converted back to time, MLX takes 0.13-0.14 ms longer in both dtypes, only a little more than the wall-clock overhead of about 0.1 ms. So the data can't show that the Enceladus kernel itself is much faster; it shows that it isn't slower.
  • PyTorch trails both. Its 161.1 and 145.3 GB/s correspond to about 0.83 and 0.46 ms, more than the overhead alone explains.
  • Reductions scale with threadgroup size. Row sums stay within 0.7% from 128 to 1,024 threads, so the reduction doesn't slow down as the threadgroup grows.
